Good Habits for Students Using AI

Use AI to help you learn, not to do the learning for you. Ask it to explain a difficult idea in simpler words, to give examples, or to quiz you on a topic. Then try the problem yourself before checking the answer. This builds understanding that you can use later, in an exam or at work.

Keep a record of how you used AI. Note which tool you used, what you asked, and how you checked the answer. This habit helps you give honest credit when required, and it makes it easier to find your way back to useful information.

Compare answers with trusted sources. Textbooks, lecture notes, official guidance, and your instructors are reliable reference points. When an AI answer disagrees with them, investigate instead of assuming either side is right.

Protect your own thinking. If you notice that you accept AI answers without understanding them, slow down. Write your own summary of a topic in your own words, and explain it aloud to yourself or a friend. Your ability to reason, question, and decide is what AI cannot replace for you.
